## The Short Version

Collecting fisheries data is difficult at scale. My thesis argues that this can be improved by developing systems for fish length measurements using tools that citizen scientists already own.

Knowing how big fish are tells us how old they are and whether a population is replacing itself, but most of that data comes from programs that need a trained scientist on hand to measure each fish. That's expensive, and it leaves much of the world's ocean unassessed. Meanwhile, hundreds of millions of anglers and millions of divers are already out where the data is needed, carrying phones and cameras.

Through [FishSense](/2023-06-20-introducing_next_generation_of_fishsense/) with [Engineers for Exploration](https://e4e.ucsd.edu/), I've worked on putting those tools to use:

- **FishSense Mobile** measures fish on deck with the depth sensor already in a phone.
- **FishSense Lite** measures fish underwater with a consumer dive camera and a dive laser. REEF volunteers used it to collect lengths paired with species on their own, with no scientist present.
- **Calibrating with commodity items** replaces specialized calibration gear with bricks at home and a dive slate at the dive site.
- **Automating a system built for people** tackles the hours of manual labeling each dive used to need, and what it takes to trust the machine that replaces it.
